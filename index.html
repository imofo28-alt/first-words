Your voice

Say it like you’d say it to him — slow, warm, sing-song. Say it twice: “Ball! … Ball.”

    Session

    How he moves between pictures

    Short and daily beats long and rare. Each session uses the least-recently practised words (three when swiping, two when tapping) and ends by itself.

    Help

    How to use it with him
    • Always together, on your lap — the app is a picture book, you are the teacher.
    • He swipes to the next picture, or taps the one he likes; the app says the word in your voice. There is no wrong answer.
    • In the two seconds of silence after the word: you say it too, warmly. That pause is for you both.
    • Afterwards, find the real thing: “Look — your ball! Same!” That bridge is what makes it stick.
    • Tick “He says it” once he uses a word in real life — check the day after a session, away from the iPad.
    Putting it on the iPad
    • The app needs to be reachable over the internet (HTTPS) for the iPad — free options like GitHub Pages or Netlify work. Ask Claude to set this up when you’re ready.
    • On the iPad, open the address in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen. It then opens full-screen like a normal app and works offline.
    • Your photos, recordings and word list live only on the device itself — nothing is uploaded anywhere.
    • Record your voice on the iPad itself (recording needs the secure HTTPS address).
    Locking the iPad to this app (Guided Access)
    • Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access → turn on, set a passcode.
    • Open First Words, then triple-click the top button and tap Start.
    • The iPad now stays in this app: home gesture and buttons do nothing.
    • To leave: triple-click again and enter your passcode. You can also set a time limit in the Guided Access options.
